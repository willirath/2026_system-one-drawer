✏️ System-One Alphabet

Draws the alphabet from toy building blocks (square A×A, double square 2A×A, half square, roof with base 2A and height A), placed face to face. Triangles turn in quarter turns; squares and double squares can also sit diagonally, against a triangle's slanted face or another diagonal block. TypeSafe's Jev (via OpenRouter), a fast decision model, is the only judge.

Each letter evolves as 10 drawings at once. Each generation, Jev sees them side by side in a grid and answers a choice question: "Which panel shows a capital letter X?" Its probabilities are the fitness. For the pick, Jev also answers a noul question: "The image shows a capital letter X." That P tracks progress. The pick and the best drawing so far (highest P) carry over unchanged. The others are children of parents drawn by fitness: half by crossover (one parent's blocks on one side of a random cut, the other's on the other side), half by mutation (1-3 blocks added, or one taken away). A drawing has at most 8 blocks. A run stops at P > 0.66 or after 30 generations and shows the best drawing.
